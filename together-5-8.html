The big idea of weeks 5 to 8

Think of it like this

In weeks 1 to 4 you built the tools (vectors, derivatives, projection, eigenvectors) and the first unsupervised algorithms. Weeks 5 to 8 are the second chapter: now we have labels, and we learn to predict. The same tools come back wearing new names:

  • least squares is a projection (MLF 3) solved with a symmetric matrix (MLF 5);
  • ridge is that same solution made safe, and SVD (MLF 6) shows exactly how it shrinks;
  • gradient descent (MLF 7) is how a computer walks to the answer, and convexity (MLF 8) is why it always arrives;
  • k-NN, trees and Naive Bayes (MLT 7, 8) are the first classifiers, and Naive Bayes needs Bayes' rule from MLF's probability weeks.

MLP then makes you type all of it in scikit-learn.

Tip

Where this sits in the exams. Quiz 2 is taken to cover weeks 5 to 8 in each of the three courses (the official pages do not print the quiz boundary, so this split is inferred from the question topics). Do the lessons in the braided order below and you prepare all three Quiz 2 papers at once. Week 4 (eigenvalues, EM) is the bridge: skim MLF week 4 first if eigenvalues feel rusty.

Need a hint?

Show the full solution

The idea

Counting skills that pay every time in Quiz 2. Number of fits in a grid search === combinations ×\times× folds (+1+1+1 refit). OvR trains KKK models, OvO trains K(K−1)/2K(K-1)/2K(K−1)/2.
