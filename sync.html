MLF + MLT, weeks 1-4, learned together

One braided path through both courses, so each idea arrives just before you need it.

Why learn them together?

Think of it like this

Imagine two friends building a treehouse. One (MLF) makes the tools: saws, hammers, rulers. The other (MLT) builds the treehouse and keeps shouting, "Now I need a saw!" If the second friend starts before the saw exists, they get stuck. If the first friend makes every tool before anyone builds anything, you forget why you made them.

So we braid: make a tool, use it straight away, make the next tool, use that. Weeks 1 to 4 of the two courses fit together almost perfectly this way.

Here is the dependency map. An arrow means "you need the left idea to understand the right one".

Your turn: 1. A shadow is a reconstruction (MLF week 3 meets MLT week 1)

In MLT week 1 the data points (0,−1), (3,5), (6,2)(0,-1),\,(3,5),\,(6,2)(0,−1),(3,5),(6,2) were centred and had first principal component w=12(1,1)\mathbf w=\frac1{\sqrt2}(1,1)w=2​1​(1,1). (a) Write the projection matrix PPP onto that line (MLF week 3). (b) Use PPP to reconstruct the centred point (0,3)(0,3)(0,3) and find the reconstruction error. (c) Say why this equals the squared distance from (0,3)(0,3)(0,3) to the line y=xy=xy=x.

Need a hint?

P=wwTP=\mathbf w\mathbf w^TP=wwT since ∥w∥=1\|\mathbf w\|=1∥w∥=1.

Show the full solution

(a) P=12(1111)P=\frac12\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\1&1\end{pmatrix}P=21​(11​11​). Check: P2=PP^2=PP2=P, PT=PP^T=PPT=P, trace =1=1=1 (rank 1). (b) P (0,3)T=12(3,3)=(1.5, 1.5)P\,(0,3)^T=\frac12(3,3)=(1.5,\,1.5)P(0,3)T=21​(3,3)=(1.5,1.5). Error vector (0,3)−(1.5,1.5)=(−1.5, 1.5)(0,3)-(1.5,1.5)=(-1.5,\,1.5)(0,3)−(1.5,1.5)=(−1.5,1.5), squared length 2.25+2.25=4.52.25+2.25=\mathbf{4.5}2.25+2.25=4.5. (c) The reconstruction is the projection onto the line, i.e. the closest point on the line, so the error is the squared perpendicular distance from the point to the line. (The other centred points: (−3,−3)(-3,-3)(−3,−3) lies on the line so its error is 0, and (3,0)(3,0)(3,0) has error 4.54.54.5 by symmetry. The average 0+4.5+4.53=3\frac{0+4.5+4.5}3=330+4.5+4.5​=3 equals the discarded eigenvalue λ2=3\lambda_2=3λ2​=3.)

Try it yourself

Try the same idea by eye: spin the axis and watch how the red "loss" sticks (the distance to the line) shrink exactly when the shadows are most spread out.

Your turn: 2. Eigenvalues are variances (MLF week 4 meets MLT week 1)

The covariance matrix is C=(6336)C=\begin{pmatrix}6&3\\3&6\end{pmatrix}C=(63​36​). Without solving a quadratic, use the trace and determinant facts from MLF week 4 to find its eigenvalues, then give the variance explained by PC1.

Need a hint?

Sum of eigenvalues = trace; product = determinant.

Show the full solution

Trace =12=12=12, determinant =36−9=27=36-9=27=36−9=27. We need two numbers with sum 121212 and product 272727: 999 and 333. So λ1=9\lambda_1=9λ1​=9 and λ2=3\lambda_2=3λ2​=3. Variance explained by PC1 =912=75%=\frac9{12}=\mathbf{75\%}=129​=75%. Note the trace is the total variance: 121212, the sum of the two feature variances 6+66+66+6. (Eigenvector for 9: (C−9I)v=0⇒v=(1,1)(C-9I)\mathbf v=0\Rightarrow\mathbf v=(1,1)(C−9I)v=0⇒v=(1,1).)

Your turn: 3. Derivative = 0 is everywhere (MLF week 2 meets MLT weeks 3 and 4)

(a) For the numbers 1, 2, 31,\,2,\,31,2,3 find the value mmm that minimises ∑(xi−m)2\sum(x_i-m)^2∑(xi​−m)2. (b) Why is that the k-means centre update? (c) A coin shows 7 heads and 3 tails; find the ppp that maximises p7(1−p)3p^7(1-p)^3p7(1−p)3.

Need a hint?

Differentiate and set to zero in both.

Show the full solution

(a) ddm∑(xi−m)2=−2∑(xi−m)=0⇒m=xˉ=2\frac{d}{dm}\sum(x_i-m)^2=-2\sum(x_i-m)=0\Rightarrow m=\bar x=\mathbf2dmd​∑(xi​−m)2=−2∑(xi​−m)=0⇒m=xˉ=2. (b) In k-means, for fixed cluster members the best centre minimises the sum of squared distances, which by (a) is the mean of the members; so the update step moves each centre to the mean. (c) Maximise ℓ=7ln⁡p+3ln⁡(1−p)\ell=7\ln p+3\ln(1-p)ℓ=7lnp+3ln(1−p): ℓ′=7p−31−p=0⇒7(1−p)=3p⇒p=0.7\ell'=\frac7p-\frac3{1-p}=0\Rightarrow7(1-p)=3p\Rightarrow p=\mathbf{0.7}ℓ′=p7​−1−p3​=0⇒7(1−p)=3p⇒p=0.7, the MLE. The same move, "set the derivative to zero", solved three different problems.

Your turn: 4. A dot product wearing a hat (MLF week 1 meets MLT week 2)

Take x=(1,2)\mathbf x=(1,2)x=(1,2) and y=(3,1)\mathbf y=(3,1)y=(3,1). (a) Compute the dot product (MLF week 1). (b) Compute the polynomial kernel k(x,y)=(x⋅y)2k(\mathbf x,\mathbf y)=(\mathbf x\cdot\mathbf y)^2k(x,y)=(x⋅y)2. (c) Verify it with the feature map ϕ(x)=(x12,2x1x2,x22)\phi(\mathbf x)=(x_1^2,\sqrt2x_1x_2,x_2^2)ϕ(x)=(x12​,2​x1​x2​,x22​).

Need a hint?

A kernel is a dot product after lifting.

Show the full solution

(a) x⋅y=3+2=5\mathbf x\cdot\mathbf y=3+2=5x⋅y=3+2=5. (b) k=52=25k=5^2=25k=52=25. (c) ϕ(x)=(1, 22, 4)\phi(\mathbf x)=(1,\,2\sqrt2,\,4)ϕ(x)=(1,22​,4), ϕ(y)=(9, 32, 1)\phi(\mathbf y)=(9,\,3\sqrt2,\,1)ϕ(y)=(9,32​,1): dot product 9+12+4=259+12+4=259+12+4=25 ✓. The idea

3-day crash version (Quiz 1 only): Day 1: MLF W2 ("Idea 3" and "Idea 4" only: gradient, linear approximation, directional derivative) + MLF W3 (projection and least squares). Day 2: MLF W4 + MLT W1. Day 3: MLT W3 (k-means) + MLT W4 (Beta posterior, EM step). Finish each day with that week's quick quiz and real exam questions.

7-day version: follow the nine sessions, two per day, with the real-question pages on Days 5 to 7.

When you finish a lesson, open Practise with real exam questions at its bottom. When you finish all nine, go to the Past papers and sit one full Quiz 1 paper per subject under a timer.
