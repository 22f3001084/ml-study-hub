Read the words of the video
  1. Imagine rolling a marble in a smooth bowl; it always finds the single lowest spot. But on a bumpy field, it might get stuck in a small dip, not the true bottom. Mathematicians call 'bowl-shaped' problems 'convex'. In these, if you find a bottom, it's the real bottom, no fake dips!
  2. Convex means 'no dents'. A convex 'set' is a shape where a straight line between any two points stays inside. A convex 'function' is like a bowl; its graph never goes above a straight chord. We check this using something called the Hessian. Convex problems are easy because any bottom you find is the true, deepest one.
  3. Let's check if a function, like 'x squared plus a times x times y plus y squared', is convex. We use a tool called the Hessian. If its special numbers, called eigenvalues, are all positive or zero, the function is convex. For our example, this happens only if 'a' is between minus two and plus two.
  4. Remember, convex sets have no dents, and convex functions are bowl-shaped. The Hessian helps us check them! For convex problems, finding any local minimum means you've found the true global minimum. This topic is super important for your exams and for understanding advanced machine learning. The one-minute story

Think of it like this

Roll a marble inside a smooth bowl. Wherever you drop it, it ends up at the one lowest point. Now roll a marble on a bumpy field. It may settle in a small dip that is not the lowest place on the field, and you would never know there was a deeper valley elsewhere.

Convex is the mathematician's word for "bowl-shaped". For a convex problem there are no fake dips: if you reach a bottom, it is the bottom. That is why convex problems are the happy, solvable corner of machine learning, and why week 7's gradient descent is trustworthy for them.

This week: what convex sets are (shapes with no dents), what convex functions are (bowls), how to check convexity quickly (using the Hessian from week 7 and positive semi-definiteness from week 5), and why it matters.

By the end of this week you can:

  • test whether a set is convex, and build convex combinations and convex hulls;
  • test whether a function is convex using the definition, the Hessian, or closure rules;
  • say what concave, strictly convex and the epigraph mean;
  • explain why a local minimum of a convex function is global.

Words you need

Words you need
  • Convex set - a shape where the straight segment between any two of its points stays inside the shape.
  • Convex combination - a mix θx+(1−θ)y\theta\mathbf x+(1-\theta)\mathbf yθx+(1−θ)y with 0≤θ≤10\le\theta\le10≤θ≤1; the points on the segment between x\mathbf xx and y\mathbf yy.
  • Convex hull - the smallest convex set that contains some given points (stretch a rubber band round them).
  • Convex function - a function whose graph never goes above the straight chord between two of its points.
  • Concave - the upside-down version: fff is concave if −f-f−f is convex.
  • Epigraph - the region on or above the graph of a function.
  • Strictly convex - convex with no flat stretches; its minimum, if it exists, is unique.

Idea 1: Convex sets

A set SSS is convex if for any x,y∈S\mathbf x,\mathbf y\in Sx,y∈S and any θ∈[0,1]\theta\in[0,1]θ∈[0,1], the point θx+(1−θ)y\theta\mathbf x+(1-\theta)\mathbf yθx+(1−θ)y is also in SSS. In words: walk in a straight line between any two points of the set and you never leave it.

The idea

Convex: a line, a plane, a filled disc, a filled ball, a square, a triangle, a half-plane {x:aTx≤b}\{\mathbf x:\mathbf a^T\mathbf x\le b\}{x:aTx≤b}, any intersection of convex sets (so a polygon cut out by several half-planes is convex).

Not convex: a ring (annulus), the outside of a disc, a crescent, a star shape, the union of two separate discs, the set of points with x2+y2=4x^2+y^2=4x2+y2=4 (just the circle line).

Useful rules:

  • Intersection of convex sets is convex.
  • Union of convex sets is generally not convex.
  • If SSS is convex and fff is a linear map, the image f(S)f(S)f(S) is convex.

Convex hull

A convex combination of points x1,…,xk\mathbf x_1,\dots,\mathbf x_kx1​,…,xk​ is ∑θixi\sum\theta_i\mathbf x_i∑θi​xi​ with all θi≥0\theta_i\ge0θi​≥0 and ∑θi=1\sum\theta_i=1∑θi​=1. The set of all convex combinations is the convex hull.

Idea 2: Convex functions

A function fff is convex if the straight chord between any two points on its graph lies on or above the graph:

f(θx+(1−θ)y)  ≤  θf(x)+(1−θ)f(y)for 0≤θ≤1f(\theta\mathbf x+(1-\theta)\mathbf y)\;\le\;\theta f(\mathbf x)+(1-\theta)f(\mathbf y)\qquad\text{for }0\le\theta\le1f(θx+(1−θ)y)≤θf(x)+(1−θ)f(y)for 0≤θ≤1

Three ways to check convexity

The idea
  1. Second derivative (one variable): convex   ⟺  f′′(x)≥0\iff f''(x)\ge0⟺f′′(x)≥0 everywhere.
  2. Hessian (many variables): convex   ⟺  \iff⟺ the Hessian is positive semi-definite at every point. (If it is positive definite everywhere, the function is strictly convex.) This is weeks 5 and 7 joining hands.
  3. First-order: fff is convex   ⟺  \iff⟺ f(y)≥f(x)+∇f(x)⋅(y−x)f(\mathbf y)\ge f(\mathbf x)+\nabla f(\mathbf x)\cdot(\mathbf y-\mathbf x)f(y)≥f(x)+∇f(x)⋅(y−x) for all x,y\mathbf x,\mathbf yx,y. The right side is exactly the linear approximation from week 2, so the statement says: the tangent line (plane) always lies below the graph.

Convexity and the epigraph

The epigraph of fff is {(x,t):t≥f(x)}\{(\mathbf x,t):t\ge f(\mathbf x)\}{(x,t):t≥f(x)}, everything on or above the graph. Then: fff is a convex function   ⟺  \iff⟺ its epigraph is a convex set. This is the link between the two halves of the week.

Building convex functions from others

  • Sum of convex functions is convex. Non-negative multiple c fc\,fcf (c≥0c\ge0c≥0) is convex.
  • Maximum of convex functions is convex. (So max⁡(0, 1−t)\max(0,\,1-t)max(0,1−t), the hinge loss, is convex.)
  • f(Ax+b)f(A\mathbf x+\mathbf b)f(Ax+b) is convex if fff is (compose with an affine map).
  • If fff is convex and ggg is convex and non-decreasing, then g(f(x))g(f(\mathbf x))g(f(x)) is convex. (E.g. efe^{f}ef.)
  • Not safe: difference of convex functions, or a product of convex functions.

Well-known convex functions: x2x^2x2, x4x^4x4, ∣x∣|x|∣x∣, exe^xex, −ln⁡x-\ln x−lnx on x>0x>0x>0, any norm ∥x∥\|\mathbf x\|∥x∥, and xTAx\mathbf x^TA\mathbf xxTAx when AAA is PSD. Not convex: x3x^3x3 on all of R\mathbb RR, sin⁡x\sin xsinx, x4−2x2x^4-2x^2x4−2x2 (two dips), −x2-x^2−x2 (that is concave).

Solved example: 2. Convex or not? Use the Hessian

Question. Decide whether (a) f(x,y)=x2+xy+y2f(x,y)=x^2+xy+y^2f(x,y)=x2+xy+y2 and (b) g(x,y)=(x+y)2g(x,y)=(x+y)^2g(x,y)=(x+y)2 are convex, and (c) for which aaa is h(x,y)=x2+axy+y2h(x,y)=x^2+axy+y^2h(x,y)=x2+axy+y2 convex.

Solution.

(a) H=(2112)H=\begin{pmatrix}2&1\\1&2\end{pmatrix}H=(21​12​), eigenvalues 3,1>03,1>03,1>0, so strictly convex.

(b) g=x2+2xy+y2g=x^2+2xy+y^2g=x2+2xy+y2, so H=(2222)H=\begin{pmatrix}2&2\\2&2\end{pmatrix}H=(22​22​) with eigenvalues 444 and 000. PSD but not PD: convex, but not strictly (it is flat along the line x+y=0x+y=0x+y=0, where it is identically 0).

(c) H=(2aa2)H=\begin{pmatrix}2&a\\a&2\end{pmatrix}H=(2a​a2​) has eigenvalues 2±a2\pm a2±a. Both are ≥0\ge0≥0 iff ∣a∣≤2|a|\le2∣a∣≤2. So hhh is convex exactly when −2≤a≤2-2\le a\le2−2≤a≤2.

Idea 3: Why convexity makes life easy

The idea

For a convex function on a convex set, every local minimum is a global minimum. Also, the set of all minimisers is itself convex, and for a strictly convex function the minimiser is unique. Moreover, ∇f(x∗)=0\nabla f(\mathbf x^*)=\mathbf 0∇f(x∗)=0 is enough to guarantee a global minimum (no Hessian check needed).

Why? Suppose x\mathbf xx is a local minimum but some other point y\mathbf yy has a lower value, f(y)<f(x)f(\mathbf y)<f(\mathbf x)f(y)<f(x). Walk a tiny fraction θ\thetaθ of the way from x\mathbf xx towards y\mathbf yy, to z=(1−θ)x+θy\mathbf z=(1-\theta)\mathbf x+\theta\mathbf yz=(1−θ)x+θy. The chord rule gives f(z)≤(1−θ)f(x)+θf(y)<f(x)f(\mathbf z)\le(1-\theta)f(\mathbf x)+\theta f(\mathbf y)<f(\mathbf x)f(z)≤(1−θ)f(x)+θf(y)<f(x). But z\mathbf zz can be as close to x\mathbf xx as we like (make θ\thetaθ small), so there are points arbitrarily near x\mathbf xx that are lower. That contradicts "x\mathbf xx is a local minimum". So no such y\mathbf yy exists.

Where do convex problems turn up?

  • Least squares ∥Xw−y∥2\|X\mathbf w-\mathbf y\|^2∥Xw−y∥2 has Hessian 2XTX2X^TX2XTX, which is always PSD: convex.
  • Ridge regression adds λ∥w∥2\lambda\|\mathbf w\|^2λ∥w∥2: strictly convex (MLT week 6).
  • The logistic loss and the SVM objective are convex (MLT weeks 9 and 10).
  • k-means and neural networks are not convex; that is why they only promise "a local minimum".

Jensen's inequality: for a convex fff, f(E[X])≤E[f(X)]f(E[X])\le E[f(X)]f(E[X])≤E[f(X)]. For example (E[X])2≤E[X2](E[X])^2\le E[X^2](E[X])2≤E[X2], which is just "variance ≥0\ge0≥0".

Exam radar

Exam radar

Convexity belongs to the End Term. Summaries mention convex functions in 21 papers, convex sets in 13, convexity in 8, convex set in 9, Hessian matrix in 13, alongside the optimisation topics of weeks 7 and 9.

Typical questions: "Which of the following functions are convex?", "For what values of aaa is this function convex?" (Hessian eigenvalues), "Which statements about convex sets are true?", "Is this point in the convex hull?", and "what does the composition of two functions look like near a point".

The newest papers (2025 to 2026) still carry convexity MSQs every time: sets, hull, epigraph and composition.

Watch out - real exam trap
  • Union of convex sets is usually not convex. Intersection is.
  • Convex function ≠\ne= "has one minimum": f(x)=0f(x)=0f(x)=0 and f(x)=(x+y)2f(x)=(x+y)^2f(x)=(x+y)2 are convex with many minimisers. Only strictly convex gives uniqueness.
  • A convex function need not have a minimum at all (exe^xex).
  • Convex means PSD Hessian (allowing zero eigenvalues), strictly convex means PD.
  • Concave ≠\ne= not convex. −x2-x^2−x2 is concave; x3−3xx^3-3xx3−3x is neither.

Quick quiz

Quick quiz

The Hessian of f has eigenvalues 3 and 0 everywhere. The function f is...

  • not convex
  • convex but not strictly convex
  • strictly convex
  • concave
All eigenvalues are at least 0, so the Hessian is PSD: convex. A zero eigenvalue means it is not strictly convex.

Which set is convex?

  • a circle line (only the boundary)
  • the union of two separate discs
  • the intersection of two discs
  • a ring (annulus)
Intersection of convex sets is convex. The others have gaps or dents.

A local minimum of a convex function is...

  • always a local maximum too
  • always a global minimum
  • never unique
  • always zero
For convex functions every local minimum is a global minimum.

Select all convex functions. (select all that apply)

  • x squared
  • |x|
  • x cubed on all of the real line
  • sin x
x cubed has a negative second derivative for x below 0, and sin x curves both ways.

Recap

Remember this
  • Convex set: segments between its points stay inside. Intersections convex, unions not.
  • Convex function: chord above graph; f′′≥0f''\ge0f′′≥0; Hessian PSD; tangent plane below graph; epigraph is a convex set.
  • Sum, non-negative multiple, max, and affine composition preserve convexity.
  • Convex ⇒\Rightarrow⇒ local minimum = global minimum, and zero gradient is enough. Least squares, ridge, logistic loss, SVM are convex; k-means and neural nets are not.
