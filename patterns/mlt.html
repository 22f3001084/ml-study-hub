Read this carefully. Within one exam date the parallel sets are drawn from the same pool, so each different question appears on several papers (the 'copies' column). Across different dates, questions with identical wording or identical figure files are rare. Matching here is exact, so a re-typed or re-cropped version counts as different; the real overlap in question type is much higher than this table can see. The takeaway: old papers teach you the types and stories that come back with new numbers, they are not a list of answers to memorise.

What each MLT paper rewards

MLT is the "algorithms" course, and the three exams split cleanly by week:

  • Quiz 1 (weeks 1 to 4): unsupervised learning and estimation. From the concept summaries: GMM in 52 of 60 papers, EM in 46, MLE in 39, Bayes in 32, PCA in 25, Bernoulli in 28, convergence in 22, covariance in 18, kernels in 15, the Beta distribution in 16, Beta posterior mean in 15, k-means++ in 10.
  • Quiz 2 (weeks 5 to 8): supervised learning I. (counts out of the 21 papers that have a summary) Decision trees (19 of 21), Naive Bayes (18), k-NN (12), MLE (12), MAP (11), information gain (11), linear regression (10), generative vs discriminative (9), kernel regression (8), ridge (7).
  • End Term (all weeks, heavy on 9 to 12). Soft-margin SVM (36 of 49 papers), hard-margin SVM (25), decision trees (24), logistic regression (23), AdaBoost (22), support vectors (20), ridge (20), overfitting (19), Naive Bayes (19), entropy (19), activation functions (19), neural networks (16), perceptron (13).

The questions that keep returning

What the papers show

Quiz 1. A Beta posterior mean after some heads and tails (the single most predictable calculation); one E-step responsibility from given likelihoods; one M-step mean or mixing weight; "does Lloyd's algorithm converge, and to what?"; k-means++ probabilities (squared distance); "which of these kernels are valid?" built from closure rules; variance explained or reconstruction error from eigenvalues; GMM parameter counts.

What the papers show

Quiz 2. Entropy and information gain from a small table; a Naive Bayes classification with Laplace smoothing; "what does a larger kkk do in k-NN?"; ridge vs LASSO (which gives zeros?); "least squares equals MLE under what noise?" (Gaussian); LOOCV on three points.

What the papers show

End Term. A fast win: MLT Quiz 1 is largely MLF Quiz 2 material applied to data. If you have done MLF weeks 3 to 6 (projection, eigenvalues, symmetric/PSD matrices, SVD/PCA), PCA and kernels are mostly vocabulary. That is the reason the weeks 1 to 4 braided plan exists.
