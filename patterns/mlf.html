What the history of past End Terms says (MLF)

Your earlier study sessions analysed 54 MLF End Term papers from 2022 to May 2026 (about 1,000 question slots, only 133 distinct stems once the parallel sets of the same exam date are collapsed). The pattern of what comes back, in tiers. (The counts below are slots: an item that sits in several parallel sets of one exam date is counted once per set.)

What the papers show

Tier 1: in almost every paper.

  • Linear programming word problems (comprehension sets): a story, then (a) the objective function (MCQ), (b) the constraints (MSQ), (c) the optimum (numeric). Recurring stories: TV sets with profits of Rs 510 and Rs 675 (seven times), products with labour and machine hours (eight times), hens and eggs, trucks vs vans, brownies vs cheesecake, a two-good consumer-utility problem (seven times).
  • KKT and Lagrange: "choose the correct primal optimisation problem" for the closest point to the origin (about 14 times), KKT true/false MSQs (about 12), complementary slackness, closest point on a circle or plane.
  • PCA: the "which of these is true about PCA?" MSQ (about 22 appearances), covariance of given points (about 11), projected variance.
  • Eigen puzzles: trace =3=3=3, determinant =2=2=2, one eigenvalue known (8 times); det⁡((A−1)T)\det((A^{-1})^T)det((A−1)T) from eigenvalues 1, 0.5, 0.251,\,0.5,\,0.251,0.5,0.25 with answer 8 (7 times); real symmetric and positive definite MSQs.
What the papers show

Tier 2: very likely. Chebyshev with μ=70, σ=8\mu=70,\ \sigma=8μ=70, σ=8, deviation 16 →0.25\to0.25→0.25 (8 times); the weak law of large numbers with exponential bulbs λ=0.4\lambda=0.4λ=0.4, n=50→0.875n=50\to0.875n=50→0.875 (6 times); Markov bounds; linear approximation L=ax+by+cL=ax+by+cL=ax+by+c at (2,3)(2,3)(2,3), find aaa (21 times); gradient-descent statements and the first/second iteration; SVD and "unitary but not Hermitian" (6 times); MLE for a normal, exponential or discrete distribution; exponential, uniform and binomial story problems.

What the papers show

Tier 3: regular. Continuity and differentiability counts, convex functions and sets (including convex hull), the fencing problem (a farmer with 120 m or a gardener with 200 m), classification vs regression, two independent coin tosses.

The trend in 2025 to 2026 papers: more applied probability stories (exponential waiting times, uniform completion times, Markov bounds on ML-flavoured numbers, a spam classification story, a least-squares house-price fit) on top of the same LP / KKT / PCA / eigenvalue core. A typical recent paper: 1 LP comprehension block, 1 KKT/Lagrange block, 1 gradient-descent block, 2 to 3 PCA/covariance items, 2 to 3 eigen/matrix items, 2 to 3 probability-inequality/MLE/distribution items, 1 to 2 calculus items.

Quiz-by-quiz, from the concept summaries on this site

  • Quiz 1 (weeks 1 to 4): linear approximation (28 of 53 papers), least squares (22), loss functions (21), dimensionality reduction and clustering (20 each), eigenvalues (20), directional derivatives (14), projection matrices (13), partial derivatives (11).
  • Quiz 2 (weeks 5 to 8; counts are out of the 30 papers that have a summary): SVD (30 of 30), PCA (29), eigenvalues (29), gradient descent (25), covariance (23), quadratic forms (19), critical points (18), Hessian (17).
  • End Term (out of 42 summarised papers): Chebyshev (23), convex functions (21), Markov (20), gradient descent (17), KKT (14), MLE (14), convex sets (13), Hessian (13).

The two four-mark closers of an End Term are usually a Lagrange multiplier problem and a least-squares problem: both are fixed recipes, so bank them first. Six rules from the earlier sessions that apply to every MLF question: (1) classify the question from its last sentence; (2) write the formula template before plugging numbers; (3) sanity-check (probabilities ≤1\le1≤1, variances ≥0\ge0≥0); (4) in MSQs, "always" and "any" flags usually make an option false; (5) re-read the rounding instruction after you finish; (6) triage: do the LP comprehension sets first, integration proofs last.
